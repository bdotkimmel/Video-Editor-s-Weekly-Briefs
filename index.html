V7 — Walk through the research

Exact script + essential actions

When an ad says nasal light therapy is backed by research, there's a question worth asking: research on what?

Because if you're buying a device for a nose that keeps blocking up, you want more than a study title next to a checkout button.

ACTION — on “Let's look at what the papers actually say.”

Bring the actual papers into view. Use the sources linked in the brief. Do not create journal covers, credentials, or lab reports.

Let's look at what the papers actually say.

ACTION — on “placebo effects could have influenced that improvement”

Highlight the relevant limitation in the single-treatment paper. Let the presenter point to it before moving to the placebo-controlled study. PMC hosting must not be labelled an NIH study.

In one study of people with allergic rhinitis, symptoms improved after a single red-light treatment. But the researchers also said placebo effects could have influenced that improvement.

That matters. Feeling better after treatment doesn't, on its own, tell us how much the treatment caused.

Which is why a placebo comparison is useful.

ACTION — on “72 percent in the treatment group”

Show a small card labelled “Reported symptom improvement: 72% treatment / 24% placebo.” On the examination result, show a separate card labelled “Endoscopic improvement: 70% / 3%.” Keep the measurements visibly distinct.

A 1997 double-blind study compared narrow-band red light with a placebo. Among the allergic-rhinitis patients, 72 percent in the treatment group reported symptom improvement, compared with 24 percent in the placebo group.

The researchers also looked inside the nose with a scope. On that examination, they found improvement in 70 percent of the treatment group, compared with 3 percent of the placebo group.

So when you see “70 versus 3,” that's the examination result. It isn't a cure rate, and it isn't the same measurement as the symptom result.

These findings give us a reason to investigate nasal light therapy. They don't make every device on the internet clinically proven.
